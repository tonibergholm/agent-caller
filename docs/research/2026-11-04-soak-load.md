# Load and soak test of acd (2026-11-04)

Status: measurements and findings. Code under test: `origin/main` at
`3404287` (the soak, stress and limit runs), plus this branch's two
`acd-core` commits where stated. Harness: `tools/loadtest/` (see its
README to rerun every number below). Raw data: `2026-11-04-soak-load/`.

## 1. Summary

| # | Finding | Evidence | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| F1 | The registry kept one `KeyState` per resolved request for the life of its agent. A long-lived agent grew the key table by one entry per request, and every resolve and `mark_gone_by_id` scanned the whole table. | Test: 8192 key states after 8192 requests on one agent. Micro-benchmark: 9 µs per open+resolve at 5k keys, 231 µs at 40k (§6.1). | **Fixed** (commit "bound the tombstones of resolved non-reusable keys", 4096 most recent) |
| F2 | The idempotency cache refuses new commands when it holds 16384 entries, and entries live 900 s. Sustained commands above about 18 per second, in total, stop all answering until entries expire or the daemon restarts. One client can fill it alone. | 10x stress: answers drop to 0 per second after about 14.3k accepted answers and stay there for 200+ s until the restart (§4). One client filled it with 16384 trivial commands in 5 s; the next client got `bad_request: too many recent commands` (§7.4). | Reported, fix proposed |
| F3 | The snapshot is one WebSocket message. Above 1 MiB (about 220 agents with 4 KB details, or 1001 agents) every client fails to sync and reconnects forever. The daemon neither bounds nor splits it. | §7.1 | Reported, fix proposed |
| F4 | The external adapter writes two INFO lines per answer (about 360 B). With launchd's unrotated `~/Library/Logs/acd.log` that is 10 MB per hour at 8 answers per second. | `log_bytes` slope in every run (§5.3) | Reported, fix proposed |
| F5 | Any local process with the hook socket can hold all 128 connection slots idle and block hook calls until each idle connection is closed (about 7 s). | §7.3 | Reported (same-user trust boundary, noted) |
| F6 | `IdemCache::begin` scans every entry per command, under the core lock: 1 µs at 100 entries, 150 to 240 µs at 16000. | §6.2 | Reported, negligible at the cap rate |

No memory, fd, thread or task leak was found in the daemon over 2 hours at
moderate load, 15 minutes at 10x, and 15 minutes of heavy reconnect churn,
other than F1 (which these runs did not exercise: see §8). Every table that
could grow with time or churn is bounded; §5.4 lists each with its bound.

## 2. Method

### 2.1 What is driven

`tools/loadtest` (a standalone crate, not a workspace member: it has its
own empty `[workspace]` table, so `cargo test --workspace` never builds or
runs it and the root `Cargo.lock` is untouched).

* **Real daemon (`soak --target acd`).** The release `acd` binary, started
  and killed as a child process, so RSS, fds, threads and CPU are the
  daemon's own (`/proc/<pid>`, every 10 s, written to CSV).
  * **Fake agents:** external producers on `hook.sock` (the producer API of
    `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-10-13-external-source-design.md`). It is the
    only source that needs no Claude session registry or `ps` check. Each
    slot is one agent: it opens a request under a fresh key, waits for a
    client's answer (long-poll), acks it (`applied`, or `rejected` or
    `failed` 3% of the time), then opens the next. Unanswered requests are
    withdrawn after 15 s (as if answered at the terminal). The daemon allows
    8 producers of 32 items, so this driver tops out at 256 concurrent
    agents. 2% of opens replace the agent by a new one (agent churn).
  * **Clients:** `acd-client` (`spawn_machine`), on `acd.sock`. Each sees
    every `request.opened`; one designated client (by request id) answers
    90% of them after 50 ms to 1.5 s; each other client also answers 2%
    (races, giving `request_busy` and `request_gone`).
  * **Churn:** every ~60 s each client drops its connection with 25%
    probability and the library resumes.
  * **Bursts:** every 5 min every free slot opens a request at once.
  * **Restarts:** every 30 min, SIGTERM and SIGKILL alternating, with
    producers re-sending their items under new keys.
* **Instrumented host (`soak --target host`).** The daemon's own engine,
  registry, idempotency cache, event ring and acd/1 server (`acd-core`
  with its `testing` feature), served on a Unix socket, with built-in
  synthetic agents instead of source adapters. It reports `Core::sizes()`
  every second, which the real daemon cannot show, supports any number of
  agents, and can burst past the 1000-event ring.
* **Latency**, both from the same process clock: the source stamps
  `t0=<unix µs>` in the request detail just before sending it.
  * *open to visible:* `t0` to a client's `request.opened` (one sample per
    client and request).
  * *answer to resolved:* a client submits the answer to the same client
    seeing `request.resolved by inbox` (includes the producer's ack turnaround).
  * *command rtt:* submit to the command result.
* Histograms are log-linear (about 6% bucket error). The CSV holds per-10 s
  window percentiles; the summary holds whole-run percentiles.

### 2.2 Machine

Intel Xeon 2.1 GHz, 4 vCPU, 16 GB RAM, no swap, Linux 6.18 microVM,
rustc 1.97, running as root (uid 0). Release build of `acd`. Loopback and
Unix sockets only. The harness and the daemon share the machine; the

applies). The sandbox has no IPv6, so the daemon listened on `127.0.0.1`

## 3. How to read the tables

`rss_MB` is the mean RSS in the 10-minute bucket, other columns the mean,
except `fds`, `thr`, `vis_max`, `res_max`, `errs`, `lag` which are the
maximum in the bucket. Latencies are milliseconds. `vis` is open to visible,
`res` is answer to resolved. `errs` is cumulative.

One uninterrupted daemon, same load as §4.1 (8 opens per second, 128

## 5. Growth and leaks

every 5 minutes a burst: every agent opens at once plus 1500 one-shot
agents (about 3300 events in under a second, past the 1000-event ring and
each session's 1000-message queue). 4 clients, churn every minute.

Whole run: 49752 `request.opened` seen, 28 injected drops but **147

### 5.2 Instrumented host, reconnect churn, 15 min

16 clients, half of them dropped every ~5 s: 1434 drops, **1432 resumes

### 5.3 Log growth

The daemon's stderr grows without bound with traffic. The external adapter
logs two INFO lines per answer (`external answer queued`, `external ack
applied`), about 360 B each pair with timestamp and ANSI colour. Measured:
374 B per answer in the soak, 357 B at 10x. In the soak that is
10.4 MB per hour (`log_bytes` slope in all four process runs: 10.32 to
10.44 MB/h), 250 MB per day at 8 answers per second; at 10x the first
200 s wrote 4.9 MB. Under systemd it goes to the journal (rotated); under
launchd the plist sends it to `~/Library/Logs/acd.log`, which launchd does
not rotate. Real use is orders of magnitude below this rate, but the log is
the only thing here that grows linearly forever. See §9, F4.

## 7. Documented limits and beyond them

All from `2026-11-04-soak-load/limits.txt` (`acd-loadtest limits`), real
`acd` unless stated.

### 7.1 Agents per client and frame size

PROTOCOL.md: a client holds at most 1000 agents per machine; no single agent
over 64 KiB; frames at most 1 MiB in both directions.

Real client (`acd-client`) against the instrumented host with N agents, each
with one open request:

| agents | detail per request | snapshot size | client |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1000 | 0 B | 688,537 B | online, 1000 agents |
| **1001** | 0 B | 689,231 B | **never syncs**; 4 reconnects in 15 s |
| 1100 | 0 B | 757,937 B | never syncs |
| 100 | 4000 B | 468,435 B | online |
| 200 | 4000 B | 937,335 B | online |
| **300** | 4000 B | 1,406,235 B | **never syncs** |
| 400, 600, 1000 | 4000 B | 1.9 to 4.7 MB | never syncs |

Beyond 1000 agents the client rejects the snapshot ("snapshot over the
agent limits"), reconnects with backoff and tries again forever; the daemon
keeps sending the whole snapshot each time. Nothing is dropped on the
daemon side and no other client is affected, but the affected client is
down for as long as the condition holds.

**F3:** the 1 MiB limit bites before the 1000-agent limit. A request is about
690 B on the wire with an empty detail and 4.7 KB with the 4000-byte detail
the external API allows, so roughly 220 agents with large pending requests
exceed 1 MiB, and then **every client** fails to sync (a full outage that
reconnecting cannot fix), while an incremental event still fits. The daemon
has no check on the snapshot size. Which sources really produce 4 KB details
at that scale was not measured (herdr screens are capped at 40 lines).
Proposed fix (bigger than a local change, so not made here): the daemon
checks the encoded snapshot against `MAX_FRAME_BYTES` and, over it, sends
the snapshot with the largest `raw_screen` and `detail` fields truncated (or
an empty snapshot followed by one `agent.updated` per agent), and logs a
bounded warning; the client keeps today's check.

Inbound frames to the daemon (WebSocket text frame, via `acd.sock`):

| frame | result |
|---|---|
| exactly 1 MiB (1,048,576 B) | accepted, `pong` returned (also 1 MiB outbound) |
| 1 MiB + 1 | connection reset by the daemon, no close frame |
| `agent.send` text 64 KiB | passes the limit (`agent_gone` for the made-up agent) |
| `agent.send` text 64 KiB + 1 | `bad_request` |
| `hello` with a 129-byte `client.id` | close 4401 "invalid hello" |

### 7.2 Pre-authentication slots

| Probe | Result |
|---|---|
| Unix socket: 12 upgrades without `hello` | 8 accepted, 4 refused with HTTP 503; the slots free after the 5 s hello timeout |
| TCP, 5 connections from one IP | 2 held, 3 closed straight away (per-IP cap 2) |
| TCP, 48 connections from 24 loopback addresses (2 each) | **16** held, 32 closed |
| the same held connections 11 s later | 0 left (5 s handshake and 10 s pre-auth deadlines) |
| a new connection after release | held (no lasting lockout) |

The documented 32 shared unauthenticated TCP connections is not reachable by
connections that have not started TLS: the 16-handshake stage cap bounds
those first, so 16 is what a flood of silent connections can hold. Not
tested: authenticated connections beyond a pre-auth stage (the 8 network
slots past the upgrade need a TLS client).

### 7.3 "128 pre-auth slots": the hook socket's 128-connection cap

The task text mentioned 128 pre-auth slots. PROTOCOL.md has no 128 for
pre-auth (8 Unix, 8 network, 32 TCP, 2 per IP). The 128 is the hook socket's
connection cap (`endpoint.rs`, `max_connections: 128`; the external spec
also cites it). Both were tested:

* 140 idle connections to `hook.sock`: the 141st request got no answer in
  3 s. The daemon closes an idle connection after about 7 s; after the
  idle connections were dropped a request was answered at once.
* F5: any process that can connect to `hook.sock` (same user) can therefore
  stall all hook calls, and Claude Code hooks wait, for about 7 s at a time,
  repeatedly. Same-user trust boundary; noted, not a regression.

### 7.4 Idempotency cache limit (F2)

One client (`client.id` `lt-flood`) sends `agent.mark_seen` for an unknown
agent with 17000 distinct ids, one at a time (4.9 s): the 16384th is the
first refused. A second client's very next command gets
`bad_request: too many recent commands; retry later`. Entries live 900 s
(`IDEM_TTL`), so that lasts up to 15 minutes, or until the daemon restarts.

Proposed fix (not made: it changes protocol-visible behaviour and needs a
decision): (a) cache only results a retry could repeat a side effect
without (delivered answers and sends), not `request_gone`, `agent_gone` and
`bad_request` results, whose retry computes the same answer; (b) cap entries
per client (for example 2048) so one client cannot take the whole table;
(c) when full, evict the oldest *done* entries older than the client retry
window instead of refusing. The client retries for 14 minutes
(`Timing::stale_inflight`), which is why the TTL is 900 s.

## 8. Not covered

* The real hook, herdr and T3 sources: they need Claude session registry
  files and `ps`, herdr or T3 Code; their per-session tables
  (`claude_sessions`, tickets, dialogs, owner) were not driven. The fake
  agents use the external producer API, which shares the core, registry,
  event ring, idempotency cache and server with them.
* The moderate soak, stress and host-less runs of §4.1 to §4.3 ran the
  `origin/main` daemon *before* the F1 fix. They did not show F1 because the
  external adapter removes an agent when it stops listing it (§4.5 control).
  F1 was found by reading the registry, confirmed by the test and §6.1, and
  reproduced on the real daemon only with agents that stay listed (§4.5).
* TLS/TCP sessions: clients used the Unix socket (the TLS stage was probed
  only for pre-auth admission). Tailscale was not available.
* The client `Inbox` and the Mac and Omarchy apps were not driven; the
  harness uses the connection library (`MachineHandle`) that they sit on.
* A test longer than 2 hours, memory fragmentation over days, and macOS.
* Two overlapping runs (§2.2) share the 4 cores; latencies of the 10x run
  and the overlap windows are an upper bound.

## 9. Findings and proposed fixes

* **F1 fixed.** `RETIRED_KEYS_CAP = 4096`. A non-reusable key keeps its
  tombstone until 4096 newer ones resolved. A replayed observation older than
  that would reopen the request; at 8 requests per second that is more than
  8 minutes, and the external adapter keeps its own tombstones (4096 per
  producer) so it is unaffected. Test
  `a_long_lived_agent_does_not_accumulate_key_states` fails on the parent
  commit (`key states grew with history: 8192`).
* **F2, F3:** see §7.4 and §7.1.
* **F4 (log growth).** Proposed: log answer queueing and ack at `debug`, or
  keep INFO but count them and log a summary once per minute; document
  `newsyslog` or log rotation for the launchd log.
* **F5, F6:** noted above.
